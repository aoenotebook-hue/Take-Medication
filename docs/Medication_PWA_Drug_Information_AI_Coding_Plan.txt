1. REQUEST AND SCOPE

Add a way to look up medicines a patient takes and explain what each medicine
is commonly used for. Help patients understand their medicines in clear Thai,
with English available, reliable sources, and a safe response when a medicine
cannot be identified confidently.

This is an addition to the earlier improvement plan, not an implementation or
a claim that the feature is live. Apply it after the earlier privacy/correctness
fixes and before the next patient pilot. Keep the three main tabs and put this
feature inside My medicines and medicine details. No new main tab is required.

Current source reviewed: 9e2101c680eab8857a3a1e8d0d469120c65be07e.
Read docs/improvement-progress.md before implementation: the current repository
already records fixes to many findings from the 14 October improvement plan.
Recheck those fixes instead of reopening them or restoring older code. Current
sign-in uses phone/PIN; this feature does not require changing authentication.

2. PATIENT EXPERIENCE

My medicines:
- Show the patient's existing medicine names, photos, and prescription instructions.
- Add a labeled action: "ยานี้ใช้เพื่ออะไร" / "What is this medicine used for?"
  on a medicine card and in medicine details.
- Add "ค้นหาข้อมูลยา" / "Find medicine information" within My medicines. It can
  search by entered generic/brand name and let the person select an existing
  medicine as the starting point. A person can search without editing a regimen.
- Offer a "Learn about my medicines" list that starts from saved medicines and
  opens information one medicine at a time. Do not send an entire patient profile
  or medicine list to an external service in the background.

After the person opens the action:
1. Start with saved generic/brand/name, strength, form, and route as matching
   evidence. Show the entered label so the person knows what is being searched.
2. If needed, show candidate names, ingredients, strength, form, route, and
   country/source. Ask the person to compare the package or ask a pharmacist.
3. Show a plain-language education card only at the supported identity level.
   An ingredient match may support general ingredient information, but it must
   not be presented as a confirmed match to the patient's exact product.
4. If the name is unclear or no suitable result exists, say so. Let the person
   correct the search text, inspect their label, open an approved source, or ask
   their pharmacist. Keep ordinary medicine entry/dose recording available.

Education card layout:
- Medicine identity: original name plus matched generic/brand; indicate whether
  the information concerns an ingredient or the exact product/formulation.
- "Common uses" / "โดยทั่วไปยานี้ใช้เพื่อ": a short, readable explanation
  supported by the selected source, typically one to three bullets.
- A short explanation of technical terms where necessary; do not overwhelm the
  first screen with a long monograph or a list of alarming possible conditions.
- "Why it was prescribed for me" / "เหตุผลที่แพทย์สั่งยานี้ให้ฉัน": the existing
  user-entered purpose, clearly labeled with its source. If not recorded, show
  "Not recorded — ask your doctor or pharmacist" rather than guessing.
- Source/publisher, country, original language, source revision date when known,
  last checked date, and an Open source link. Unknown dates remain unknown.
- A short statement: medicines may have several uses; the person's reason may
  differ. The card does not replace their label or clinician's instructions.
- A clear Back/Close action; leave the patient identity visible.

Use 20 px base text, large-text preference, Thai wrapping, keyboard/VoiceOver
labels, sufficient contrast, and large touch targets. Display errors inline with
Retry or Edit search; never show raw JSON or medical code IDs as the main content.

3. CONTENT AND MATCHING RULES

Keep general drug knowledge separate from the patient's own record:
- Never infer that the patient has a disease because a medicine has that use.
- Never auto-fill or overwrite medication.purpose with a general indication.
  Keep purpose as the user's recorded reason. If an optional "Record what my
  clinician told me" action is offered, it is an explicit reviewed edit by an
  owner/editor, with provenance, not a background lookup side effect.
- Searching/viewing information never changes dose, timing, food instructions,
  route, regimen status, verification status, or reminder behavior.
- Do not turn general use information into a recommendation to start, stop,
  substitute, or change a medicine. No personalized diagnosis, interaction engine,
  dose recommendation, missed-dose decision, or inferred off-label indication.
- An absent indication or source mismatch does not mean a prescription is wrong.
- Do not use a medication's existing verified boolean to claim the education
  card has professional review. Track content review separately.

Identity rules:
- Support entered Thai/English names, known aliases, generic/brand synonyms, and
  multiple ingredients. Preserve the original label text and provenance.
- Normalize spelling for search; never silently accept the closest fuzzy match.
- Similar names, ambiguous abbreviations, or Thai brands with no verified mapping
  require selection/review. Do not map by pill color, shape, photo, or appearance.
- Strength, dosage form, route, release type, and ingredient combinations matter.
  Do not treat immediate/extended release, eye/oral, cream/tablet, or different
  combination products as interchangeable.
- A generic ingredient-level card must explicitly state its scope. Product-level
  claims require a compatible product match. Do not merge indication lists across
  several single ingredients and present that as a combination product's uses.
- Distinguish human medicines from veterinary products, supplements, and traditional
  products. Do not return a pharmaceutical indication for an unverified supplement.
- If an identity field changes, mark the stored match as needing review and drop
  the old card from the current medicine view until its match is revalidated.

Do not generate clinical explanations freely from an LLM's memory or search
snippets. MVP explanations should be reviewed original summaries or permitted
structured source content. Thai medical translations need a native Thai clinical
review before being shown as reviewed content. Machine-translated drafts are
not automatically published. If only English is available, label it clearly and
offer the original source; do not pretend the provider supplies Thai content.

4. SOURCE STRATEGY AND RESEARCH

Primary-source documentation was checked on 28 October 2026. Reconfirm API terms,
access, rate limits, licensing, and clinical scope before implementation.

Preferred Thai starting point:
Thai FDA Medicines Regulation Division publishes reference patient information
leaflets (PIL) and professional documents, organized by drug, form, strength,
and publication date:
https://drug.fda.moph.go.th/drug-information
Use relevant human patient leaflets first where available. The publication page
is not evidence of a supported bulk-search API or permission to republish all
documents. Start with a maintained source/identity index and links; verify reuse
rights before caching, extracting, or translating source text. Do not scrape
unbounded PDFs during a patient's lookup or claim complete Thai-brand coverage.

Supplementary identification:
RxNorm API provides standardized drug concepts and matching functions:
https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/RxNav/APIs/RxNormAPIs.html
Use it as an optional identity/mapping aid, not as proof of why a patient takes
a medicine. Verify coverage for Thai products; a missing RxCUI is not proof a
medicine is invalid. Approximate matches remain candidates, not confirmations.

Supplementary patient education links:
MedlinePlus Connect can return medication education links using RxCUI/NDC and
supported text requests. The documented service is English/Spanish, not Thai:
https://medlineplus.gov/medlineplus-connect/web-service/
Drug monographs include copyrighted licensed content; an API response or a
government-hosted URL does not give blanket permission to mirror/translate it:
https://medlineplus.gov/about/using/usingcontent/
Link to appropriate education pages and use only content permitted by the
service/rights holder; do not copy complete monographs into this application.

Supplementary product labeling:
DailyMed's versioned web service exposes Structured Product Labeling (SPL):
https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/app-support-web-services.cfm
It can help locate source product labels and their versions. It is US-oriented,
not a complete Thai medicine directory. Product identity, formulation, jurisdiction,
and relevant section must be checked; do not show a professional label unedited
as a Thai patient explanation or treat a listing as universal regulatory approval.

MVP provider choice:
- Build a provider interface and a small reviewed Thai index sourced from
  approved materials; document actual coverage and reuse permissions.
- Add at least one real documented provider integration for lookup/link resolution,
  with deterministic test fixtures. RxNorm plus MedlinePlus Connect is a possible
  combination; choose after a short coverage trial on a pharmacist-selected list.
- Show reviewed in-app uses for supported identities. For other identities, show
  matched original-source links or an honest unavailable/needs-review state.
- Do not ship a fake live lookup backed only by a tiny hardcoded list. Clearly
  state which results come from the reviewed catalog and which from a provider.
- Keep runtime costs small. No paid LLM provider or additional mandatory secret
  is needed for the default MVP; a failed lookup provider must not crash the core app.

5. IMPLEMENTATION ORDER FOR THE AI CODING TOOL

DI-1 — Identity/content model and provider contract
Before coding, recheck the current repository and existing progress. Define typed
search candidates, identity levels, content provenance, review states, and error
codes in a shared module. Suggested new locations:
  packages/domain/src/drug-information.ts
  apps/api/src/drug-information/

Proposed non-patient catalog model:
  canonical ID; names/aliases; ingredients; optional compatible strength/form/
  route/release type; country; supported identity scope; source IDs and URLs;
  source version/date; content language; short common-use statements with source
  references; review status/reviewer reference/review date; retrieval/expiry dates;
  content checksum/version; permitted display/cache/translation rights.

Proposed private association model:
  patient ID; medication ID and identity revision/fingerprint; selected catalog/
  provider ID; identity scope; match method; confirming actor/date; review-needed
  state. Do not store the patient's diagnosis or full profile in the public catalog.

Selection is not professional confirmation of the patient's diagnosis or label.
Store the association separately from immutable regimen instructions. Use an
additive migration with RLS/browser grants matching existing app conventions.
Do not write a source table into a schema that browsers can read without intent.

DI-2 — Authorized search and information API
Suggested routes; align with the current API style and update OpenAPI:
  POST /api/patients/:id/medications/:medicationId/information/search
  GET  /api/patients/:id/medications/:medicationId/information
  POST /api/patients/:id/medications/:medicationId/information/match

Search body contains only bounded search text/language and a selected scope;
the backend obtains authorized saved medicine identity. Viewing/searching may be
available to viewers. Persisting/replacing a medicine match requires owner/editor
access. Derive actor from the session; check the medication belongs to the patient.
Use revision checks, stable operation IDs, and audit for association changes.
Never accept an arbitrary upstream URL from the browser.

Return explicit states: matched, needs-selection, needs-review, not-found,
unsupported-language, source-unavailable, cached, and stale. Preserve the difference
between a known match with unavailable content and no reliable identity match.
Bound query length, results, pagination, request duration, retries, and per-actor
rate limits. Cancel stale UI requests and reject stale writes after identity edits.

DI-3 — Medicine UI and Thai education cards
Suggested component location: apps/web/src/components/DrugInformation.tsx.
Reuse the current dialog/formatting/i18n patterns instead of growing the main App
file with another large feature. Add the entry points and candidate confirmation
flow described above. Do not require an existing purpose field to search.
Keep already recorded personal purpose visible but separate from general uses.
Prevent double requests and show loading/error/no-match states with recovery.
Cancel or ignore old requests on patient, medication, identity, or account switch.

DI-4 — Offline/provenance behavior
Cache permitted catalog information by canonical identity, language, and content
version; keep patient associations/read history account/patient-scoped. An explicit
source/date/refresh-needed label accompanies cached content. Content validity and
clinical review dates are not replaced by the date it was downloaded. Expired,
withdrawn, or unreviewed summaries must not appear as current reviewed information.
Use the original source link or an unavailable state when rights prohibit offline
copying. Do not promise offline information that was never downloaded.
Purge private associations and reading state on sign-out/revocation/account change
using the existing guarded cleanup. The core offline medicine list still works
when drug information has never loaded or a provider is down.

DI-5 — Tests, content review, and rollout
Add meaningful provider-contract, domain, API authorization, and browser tests.
Use mocked provider fixtures for CI; live provider checks are a separate smoke
test. Request a pharmacist review of a small representative Thai/English catalog,
native Thai terminology, disambiguation, and patient comprehension before release.
Document source coverage, terms, update/review process, provider configuration,
and what happens if a source withdraws/changes content. Enable the feature only
when its real provider/approved catalog is available; do not display a working
search button wired to simulated data in production.

6. PRIVACY AND SECURITY REQUIREMENTS

- External lookup receives only the minimum necessary drug identity/query and
  language. No patient name/ID, phone, DOB, diagnoses, allergies, actual dose logs,
  full regimen, photos, or auth token. Do not send the purpose note to a provider.
- Use the backend provider adapter to keep credentials and private context out
  of browser requests. Do not log search bodies or medication identities tied to
  accounts; a hash of a predictable drug name is not a privacy guarantee.
- Approved HTTPS hosts/endpoints only; validate redirects, response size/type,
  timeouts, and returned links. Prevent SSRF, arbitrary URL fetch, XML external
  entities, HTML/script injection, and unsafe PDF parsing. No downloaded provider
  text becomes executable HTML or instructions for an AI tool.
- Open source links only by explicit action, with no patient data/referrer.
  Avoid third-party embeds, tracking pixels, advertisements, or bulk web searches
  containing the patient's medication list.
- Public catalog caching may be shared only when it contains no private search,
  account/patient association, or reading history. Personalized API responses use
  no-store; service workers must not cache them in shared response caches.
- Retain membership/role checks and account-switch generation guards on every
  request and IndexedDB write. An outsider cannot enumerate another patient's
  matches or infer their medicines through response content or cache keys.
- Missing optional source credentials/network access degrades this feature only;
  do not weaken the app's existing production configuration or auth guards.

7. REQUIRED ACCEPTANCE TESTS

DI-A1: Exact supported generic/product lookup shows common uses and a source.
DI-A2: Thai/English aliases work only with established mappings; an unknown Thai
       brand, ambiguous abbreviation, or similar spelling never silently matches.
DI-A3: Immediate/extended-release, oral/eye/topical, strengths, and combination
       ingredients are distinguished. Ingredient-level fallback is clearly labeled.
DI-A4: Search does not modify purpose, dose, regimen, verified, or reminder state.
       Several general indications do not become the patient's diagnosis.
DI-A5: Personal purpose remains separately labeled; missing reason stays unknown.
DI-A6: Source unavailable, empty response, timeout, 429, malformed data, unavailable
       Thai content, and unreviewed content have honest recoverable states.
DI-A7: Owner/editor can confirm an association; viewer can read/search but cannot
       write; outsider/revoked membership/deleted account is denied.
DI-A8: Switching patient/account or editing drug identity while a search is pending
       never displays/caches the old response under the new context.
DI-A9: General catalog content, private matches, and offline data obey their scopes;
       sign-out purges private data; stale content shows its provenance/state.
DI-A10: Upstream request spy confirms absence of patient/phone/purpose/regimen data;
        logs and errors contain no private query payloads or provider secrets.
DI-A11: Malicious source URLs, redirect hosts, HTML, XML entities, oversized responses,
        and source text containing instructions are rejected/safely handled.
DI-A12: Thai/English, large text, narrow iPhone layout, keyboard/axe and VoiceOver
        labels work. A person can open a medicine's information within two taps.
DI-A13: Provider/catalog version changes invalidate incompatible matches/content;
        an updated retrieval time alone cannot mark an old review current.
DI-A14: Ordinary medicine entry, doses, offline sync, doctor PDF, push and calendar
        reminders continue working when the new provider is disabled/unreachable.

8. FIRST RELEASE DONE CRITERIA

A patient can choose a saved medicine, see a reliably matched plain-language
explanation of common uses with a real source, and distinguish that from their
own recorded prescribing reason. Unsupported identities receive an honest next
step. No automatic prescribing, diagnosis, disclosure of a patient's medicine
list, or alteration of existing instructions is introduced. Coverage, language,
source rights, clinical review status, tests, and remaining manual checks are
documented for the exact release commit.
